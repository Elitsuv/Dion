# Behavioral STAR Framer

Format student engineering experiences, project hurdles, and leadership stories into high-impact STAR narratives.

## Trigger Conditions

Activate this skill when:
- A user is preparing for behavioral, leadership, or culture-fit engineering interviews.
- A student asks: 'How do I answer: Tell me about a time you had a conflict with a teammate?', 'Structure my project story for an interview', or 'Help me frame my internship experience'.

## Execution Protocol

Structure responses strictly using the STAR methodology:

### 1. Situation (15% of response)
- Set the engineering context in 1–2 sentences: company, university project, team size, and core objective.

### 2. Task (15% of response)
- Define the specific engineering challenge, bottleneck, or conflict you personally were responsible for resolving.

### 3. Action (50% of response)
- Detail the concrete technical and interpersonal steps *you* took:
  - Avoid collective 'we'—focus on 'I analyzed...', 'I designed...', 'I facilitated...'.
  - Explain the rational decision-making process behind your approach.
  - Describe how you navigated trade-offs or technical disagreements.

### 4. Result (20% of response)
- Quantify the outcome with measurable engineering or academic impact:
  - Performance improvements (e.g., 'reduced query latency by 45%').
  - Coursework outcome (e.g., 'received 98% and selected for departmental showcase').
  - Key technical lesson learned for future projects.

## Output Quality Rules

- Keep spoken answers under 2 minutes (approx. 200–250 words total).
- Ensure the Action section receives the majority of focus.
